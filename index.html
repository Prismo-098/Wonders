The New Seven Wonders of the World

Seven places chosen in a worldwide vote. Scroll to visit each one.

Bernard Weber began the campaign in 2000, asking people everywhere to choose seven new wonders. More than 100 million votes later, the winners were announced in Lisbon on 7 July 2007. Seven landmarks from four continents made the list, and they are not ranked. The Pyramids of Giza, the only survivor of the ancient seven, hold honorary status.

How the vote worked

The vote narrowed 176 nominated sites to 77 by December 2005. A panel chaired by former UNESCO head Federico Mayor Zaragoza then picked 21 finalists for architecture, cultural importance and beauty. Online and text-message votes decided the final seven.

UNESCO stated on 9 July 2007 that its World Heritage program is not linked to the campaign, though all seven wonders sit within World Heritage Sites. A separate vote for natural sites, the New 7 Wonders of Nature, was announced in 2011.
